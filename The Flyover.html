Seniors To Get Checks for Medicare Part B

President Trump announced on Friday that his administration would begin sending $90 checks to more than 20 million seniors to help offset the cost of Medicare Part B premiums. 

A White House fact sheet said the money will come from the Medicare Improvement Fund, with most eligible recipients receiving the payments by direct deposit in early October. 

In 2026, the standard Medicare Part B monthly premium is $202.90, while the annual deductible is $283, according to the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services.

The administration is separately distributing $500 payments to nearly 1 million Americans who purchased health insurance through the Affordable Care Act, claiming the Biden administration overcharged on user fees.

Man Learns Birth Father is Music Legend

A 59-year-old St. Louis resident recently learned that his birth father is rock music legend Michael McDonald. 

Michael Goessling received a letter from his biological parents asking to make contact after one of his half-brothers found him through a 23andMe DNA test. 

After meeting his birth mother, Goessling received a call from the Doobie Brothers’ lead singer the same night, knowing only that his father was a touring musician named “Mike.” Goessling said, “And then I finally said, ‘Are you, Michael McDonald?’” And he said, ‘Yeah, I’m afraid so.’” 

McDonald and his middle school sweetheart had a child when they were 14 years old before giving up the baby in a closed adoption.

LIST: Best Grocery Stores

The Fresh Market has been crowned America’s best grocery store in the 2026 USA Today 10Best Readers’ Choice Awards, capping a 44-year climb from its North Carolina roots.

Ray and Beverly Berry founded the chain in 1982, inspired by European food markets. Today, it runs 172 stores across 22 states, each with multiple butchers on staff cutting meat fresh every day.

Shoppers noticed the difference. The Fresh Market also swept first place in Best Produce, Best Deli, Best Prepared Foods, and Best Bakery. One fan singled out its Peppercorn Steak Market meal kit.

Hy-Vee, Lidl, ALDI, and Albertsons rounded out the top five.

Whatever happened to Yoo-hoo?

For decades, the little brown bottle with the yellow label was everywhere: corner stores, vending machines, Little League dugouts, school lunchboxes. Yoo-hoo was the chocolate drink that tasted like chocolate milk without actually being chocolate milk, and millions of American kids never thought twice about the difference.

New Jersey grocery store owner Natale Olivieri created the drink in 1928 after watching his wife boil jars of homemade tomato sauce. He figured the same heat-and-seal trick could keep a chocolate drink from spoiling on the shelf. It worked.

By the 1950s, Yankees legend Yogi Berra was appearing in TV ads, visiting the factory floor in Carlstadt, New Jersey, and eventually becoming a company vice president. His teammates, reportedly including Mickey Mantle and Whitey Ford, pitched in too, and the Yankees promoted it as “the drink of champions.”

Pepsi tried to knock off Yoo-hoo off in 1966 with a rival called Devil Shake, but discovered Yoo-hoo held exclusive rights to the sterilization equipment it needed. Pepsi reportedly lost about $5 million and sold the whole operation to Yoo-hoo for $1. Meanwhile, the Yoo-hoo ads just kept rolling out, like this animated commercial from the 1970s.

The drink hit its cultural peak in the 1990s, when it became a fixture of gas station coolers and after-school snacking alongside Dunkaroos and Capri Sun. Seinfeld cemented its place in pop culture in 1992 when Brian Doyle-Murray’s sad-sack Yoo-hoo truck driver told Jerry, “It’s a fine product.”

Today Yoo-hoo still sits on shelves at Walmart, Kroger, and Amazon, now owned by Keurig Dr Pepper. It technically contains no liquid milk, just whey, dry milk, and a lot of high-fructose corn syrup.

Its biggest recent move is a Ghost-branded protein drink collaboration that sold out online in 20 minutes. The bottle has changed, but the nostalgia hasn’t.
